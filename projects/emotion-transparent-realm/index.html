Emotion: The Transparent Realm

What if an AI exposed the feelings you chose to keep private? Emotion: The Transparent Realm is a narrative VR game in which everyday attempts to protect a relationship turn into physical encounters with emotional exposure.

Project Lead: Tingxiao Wu

Virtual Reality · Critical Design · Narrative Interaction

WHEN PRIVATE FEELINGS BECOME PUBLIC

Our preliminary survey received 108 responses from 120 questionnaires distributed. It revealed a useful tension: respondents were more willing to know others' emotions than to let others know their own. Close relationships were also a particular focus of curiosity.

We turned that asymmetry into a fictional premise: an AI reveals feelings without leaving the person in control of when or how to express them. Would complete emotional transparency bring people closer, or make intimacy harder to sustain?

CHOOSING A PLAYABLE SOCIAL CONFLICT

We compared three concepts: a survival game about detecting inconsistencies, an abstract experience in which emotions physically surround the player, and a narrative about maintaining social boundaries. The alternatives introduced heavy text, additional sensing equipment, or an incomplete story.

We chose the social-boundaries concept because it supported a complete narrative and understandable controller interactions. Friendship, family, and romantic partnership became three levels, each built around a familiar mismatch between a momentary feeling and the wish to care for someone.

TURNING DISCLOSURE INTO ACTION

The protagonist, Lamo, listens to a frustrated friend, receives a call from his mother, and responds to a handmade gift from his partner. In each encounter, a private feeling conflicts with a considerate response. The AI exposes that feeling, and the resulting emotions become figures that attack the player.

The repeated loop moves from responding to a person, to dodging an attack, searching for the source of exposure, and destroying it. Controllers support gestures, grabbing, searching, and smashing. Emotion figures collected across the three levels become the player's means of confronting the final boss.

BUILDING THE VR EXPERIENCE

We developed a first-person VR prototype with controller navigation, object interaction, NPCs that face the player, and animated emotion figures. These systems had to work together while the player moved between conversation, searching, and attack sequences.

Testing in an XR simulator exposed inconsistent scene lighting, problems with viewpoint tracking, and obscured text. We corrected these before testing with a VR headset. Matching the visual tone across scenes took further iteration so that the story could remain coherent as the setting changed.

WHAT THE FIRST PLAYTEST REVEALED

We invited three players to try the prototype. They responded positively to the theme and interactivity. One recognized the everyday dilemma of choosing between honesty and protecting a relationship; another found that some conflicts escalated too abruptly to justify the emotional outburst.

The feedback pointed to two priorities for a next iteration: develop the lead-in to each conflict and give audio a stronger role in communicating changes in mood. This was a small formative playtest intended to guide the game, rather than measure changes in players' attitudes.
