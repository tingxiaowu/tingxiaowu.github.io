Aetherus

A tangible cultural-computing concept for comparing Feng Shui and local-space astrology.

Overview

Aetherus explores how two culturally distinct systems for interpreting place—Chinese Feng Shui and Western local-space astrology—might be encountered through one tangible interface. Users provide personal context, point the camera toward a room or site, and switch among Feng Shui, astrology, and combined views. The device is framed as a cultural interpretation tool: it presents contrasting recommendations and lets users compare how different frameworks describe the same environment.

Interaction

The central control is a rotatable adjustment ring. Turning the ring switches interpretive modes and changes the visual language of the interface. After scanning a space, a user can unfold recommendations, highlight a suggested spatial change, and toggle a visual preview. When idle, the object becomes an ambient display inspired by traditional talisman forms.

Technical Prototype

The proof of concept connects an ESP32-CAM to a cloud-based multimodal model. A captured image is encoded and sent for text-based interpretation, while a separate interface prototype demonstrates mode switching and the presentation of recommendations. The architecture explores edge–cloud interaction but is not a validated environmental-analysis system.

Scope and Reflection

Aetherus is speculative cultural interaction design. Feng Shui and astrology are interpretive belief systems; the device does not scientifically measure luck, energy, or environmental quality. A future iteration could focus less on producing an authoritative answer and more on supporting comparison, reflection, provenance, and conversation around different ways of understanding place.
