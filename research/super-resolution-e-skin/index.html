Super-Resolution Tactile Sensing through Geometric Encoding in Liquid-Metal Microchannels

Shaoyu Cai†, Jiarui Feng†, Tingxiao Wu†, Hao Zheng, Xu Cheng, Kening Zhu, and Benjamin C. K. Tee
† Equal contribution; co-first authors

npj Flexible Electronics · Major Revision

Overview

High-resolution tactile perception is important for dexterous manipulation and immersive human-machine interaction, yet conventional tactile skins typically achieve fine spatial resolution by increasing sensor density, with corresponding costs in wiring, readout, and structural complexity. Here we present a thin liquid-metal electronic skin that achieves super-resolution touch localisation and normal-force sensing from only four resistive channels by encoding contact information directly into microchannel geometry. A kernel-based fast simulator predicts topology-dependent resistance responses without repeated full-field finite-element simulations, enabling noise-aware optimisation of channel geometry for spatial distinguishability. The selected interlocked topology balances spatial sensitivity and fingerprint separability, while a hierarchical mixture-of-experts (HMoE) decoder jointly estimates position and force. Across two independently fabricated devices, the sensor achieved a mean localisation RMSE of 0.223 mm, demonstrating reproducible sub-millimetre localisation. A representative device reaches 0.228 mm localisation RMSE and a force RMSE of 0.043 N, corresponding to a super-resolution factor of 58.3. Force-modulated handwriting and a wearable pressure-tiered keyboard further demonstrate continuous and discrete human-machine interaction, with the keyboard achieving 95.38% joint key-and-mode classification accuracy. These results establish simulation-guided geometric encoding as a route to high-resolution tactile sensing with sparse readout and a thin, compliant form factor.
