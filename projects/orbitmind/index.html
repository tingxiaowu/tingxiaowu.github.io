OrbitMind: Redefining Focus Through AI and Inclusive Design

This is the final project for the NUS course “CDE5302: Design Thinking and Product Development.” OrbitMind explores how an AI-assisted mobile experience could support attention, task execution, and the capture of ideas for people with ADHD.

Team Project: Cai Yimeng, Ma Xiaotain, Tao Yuheng, Wang Mengqi, Tingxiao Wu, and Xu Jiayang

Inclusive Design · AI Product Concept · Interaction Prototyping

Explore the interactive UI prototype ↗

WHY CONVENTIONAL TOOLS LOSE THE THREAD

OrbitMind begins with a mismatch between fluctuating attention and conventional productivity tools. Repetitive, text-heavy interfaces can make it difficult to initiate routine work, sustain attention, and return to an interrupted task.

At the same time, ideas may arrive quickly but remain scattered across conversations, screenshots, notes, and unfinished reminders. The project therefore treats task execution and inspiration capture as one connected design problem.

REFRAMING ATTENTION AS A DESIGN PROBLEM

The project considers two experiences often described together but supported poorly by conventional productivity tools: difficulty sustaining attention and a strong flow of ideas that can be hard to organize or act on.

We translated this into four goals—attract attention, sustain attention, reduce cognitive load, and capture inspiration—and three design principles: interest, motivation, and simplicity. This framework guided the experience instead of treating a generic chatbot as the product.

ONE INTERFACE CONNECTS THE WHOLE LOOP

OrbitMind organizes planning, execution, and reflection as a continuous cycle. The interface brings together the Star Voyage home, task planets, a Pomodoro timer, reminders, schedule management, inspiration capture, and focus summaries.

Each task becomes a planet. Completing work grows the user's universe through new planets and rewards, while overdue work introduces a visual disruption. A focus timer connects execution to that visual feedback, and the daily schedule turns a broad workload into a clearer next action.

FROM SCATTERED INPUTS TO A NEXT ACTION

The AI Memo Assistant extracts and summarizes task-relevant information from a conversation. The Idea Organizer accepts speech or sketches, turns them into a concise record, and helps connect related fragments. The Task Decomposer converts a broad assignment into smaller steps and places them into a schedule.

The technical concept maps these actions to speech transcription, language-model summarization, semantic classification, reminders, and synchronized storage. The value is the handoff between services: capture something quickly, understand it, and turn it into an action that can be started.

MAKING PROGRESS AND IDEAS VISIBLE

The same universe metaphor extends beyond task completion. Generated planet names, forms, and stories give completed work a distinct identity; focus analytics summarize patterns over time; and an association map shows how captured ideas relate to one another.

These views support different moments: motivation before a task, feedback after a focus session, and reflection when returning to earlier ideas. They are presented as an AI-service architecture and interaction prototype, rather than evidence that the proposed models were clinically evaluated.

THE CONCEPT IN AN EVERYDAY SCENARIO

The final usage scenario places the interface in a day-to-day setting. Information captured from messages can become a reminder; a broad task can be decomposed into smaller actions; a focus session produces visible progress; and saved ideas remain available for later reflection.

The outcome is an interaction and AI-service concept connecting these moments within one visual system. A next iteration would test where automation genuinely reduces effort, where it creates interruption, and how much control users want over task decomposition and reminders. OrbitMind does not claim to diagnose or treat ADHD.
