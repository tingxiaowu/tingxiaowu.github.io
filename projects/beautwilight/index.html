Beautwilight

Beautwilight is an individual design exploration of how people in hospice contexts might revisit meaningful places, share experiences with family, and preserve personal stories through VR.

Individual Project: Tingxiao Wu

Immersive Media · Service Design · Experience Prototyping

MAKING ROOM FOR PERSONAL WISHES

The starting point was a tension between personal wishes and physical access. A meaningful experience might be a journey, a familiar place, or simply time with family, yet illness can make these experiences harder to arrange. Beautwilight explores a virtual setting that can be adapted to the person rather than asking everyone to enter the same world.

The design question was how to connect a person's wishes, shared activities, and memories in one coherent experience.

FROM NEEDS TO DESIGN DECISIONS

The concept is organized around three priorities: personalization, family connection, and low-burden operation. Personalization motivates configurable environments; connection motivates shared spaces and a story archive; low-burden operation motivates a small set of recognizable entry points.

This makes the interface a way to choose an experience, not a long sequence of tasks to complete. The four modules offer different ways to participate depending on the person's interests and energy.

FOUR WAYS TO PARTICIPATE

Explore proposes visits to existing environments and the creation of a personalized world from a text prompt. Healing, the module's original name, brings together calm environments and reflective activities; it is not a claim of therapeutic effectiveness.

Play proposes lightweight games and movement-oriented experiences. Family connects shared virtual activities with a memory archive organized around places, dates, images, and stories. Together, the modules link experiencing something now with preserving something for later.

PROTOTYPING THE JOURNEY

I developed a high-fidelity interface concept and a usage scenario to communicate how these parts fit together. The proposed journey moves from choosing or describing a place, to entering an environment, participating in an activity, and revisiting saved memories.

The video presents the intended experience. It should be read as a concept walkthrough, not as evidence that scene generation, multi-user participation, and a cloud story library have all been deployed as a complete service.

WHAT THE CONCEPT ESTABLISHES

The outcome is a service and interface concept connecting personal wishes, immersive experiences, and family memory-making. Its contribution is the organization of that journey; clinical benefit and suitability for hospice use have not been established.

The next design questions concern who chooses and shares a memory, how someone can pause or leave an experience, and what level of interaction remains comfortable. These decisions would need to be developed with patients, family members, and hospice professionals.
