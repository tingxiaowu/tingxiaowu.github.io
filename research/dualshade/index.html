DualShade: A Fail-Safe Dual-Layer Adaptive Eyewear for Real-Time Glare and Luminance Control

This is the final project for my master program's course---"CDE5312: Beyond the Surface: Exploring Interactive Wearables", and in the 2025 NUS EDIC showcase, it got the best prototype award.

Project Lead: Tingxiao Wu · Collaborators: Yuheng Tao and Shaoyu Cai

Wearable Interaction · Embedded Sensing · Research Prototype

BACKGROUND

Sudden luminance changes and localized glare can briefly make visual information difficult to read. Existing solutions usually address only part of this problem: sunglasses darken the entire view, photochromic lenses may react slowly, and opaque visors can hide useful visual cues.

DualShade asks how wearable optics can respond to both global brightness and directional glare while remaining transparent when sensing is uncertain.

DESIGN CONCEPTION

The concept separates brightness control into two transparent layers. Layer 1 provides smooth, scene-wide electro-optic dimming during rapid luminance transitions. Layer 2 uses a transparent LCD mask to attenuate one of three directional zones when a localized glare source appears.

By decoupling these responses, the system can reduce a bright hotspot without unnecessarily darkening the rest of the wearer's field of view.

HOW IT WORKS

A TSL2591 sensor measures global illuminance, while three BH1750 sensors sample directional light. An ESP32 combines these readings and controls the two optical layers.

A confidence-gated policy is central to the design. When ambient light is low or the directional reading is uncertain, the eyewear returns to a transparent state so that the intervention does not create a new visibility risk.

MAKING PROCESS

We integrated the optical layers, four light sensors, and control electronics into a wearable frame, then iterated the thresholds and timing through controlled lighting changes. The making process focused on keeping the sensing directions legible, aligning the three LCD zones with the field of view, and coordinating the layers without visible control conflict.

The final demonstration supports four states: transparent, global dimming, localized masking, and a combined response.

PROTOTYPE EVALUATION

Bench testing measured an approximately 390 ms response for Layer 1 and approximately 217 ms activation and 167 ms release for Layer 2. Layer 1 reduced measured light by approximately 41%; using both layers produced approximately 67% attenuation.

In a nine-person readability task, the prototype received an average score of 5.3/7. In a separate 21-person scenario survey, all participants wanted critical visual information to remain visible, and 15 indicated willingness to use the concept. These results are early prototype evidence rather than a road-safety validation.
