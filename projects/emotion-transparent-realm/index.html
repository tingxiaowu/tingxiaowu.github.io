Emotion: The Transparent Realm

This narrative VR game explores emotional disclosure, privacy, and the boundaries of intimacy in a world where AI can reveal feelings that people have chosen not to express.

Project Lead: Tingxiao Wu

Virtual Reality · Critical Design · Narrative Interaction

Watch gameplay video ↗

BACKGROUND

Emotion-recognition technologies are often framed as tools that make communication clearer. Yet emotional opacity can also protect privacy, give people time to reflect, and help relationships tolerate uncertainty.

The project asks a critical question: does making every emotion visible necessarily create a more honest or caring relationship?

GAME CONCEPTION

I translated this question into a first-person story in which hidden feelings become visible characters. These figures interrupt a conversation, confront the player, and gradually expose the system responsible for making private emotion public.

The experience moves through three relationship-based levels—friendship, family, and romantic partnership—before a final confrontation. Each stage introduces a familiar social tension and asks the player to balance honesty, self-protection, and the expectations that sustain a close relationship.

INTERACTION DESIGN

Tracked controllers turn emotional conflict into embodied action. Players move through the scene, search for objects, grab and examine clues, comfort characters through gesture, dodge emotion figures, and face the final source of exposure.

The interaction rhythm shifts deliberately from ordinary conversation to increasingly visible and uncontrollable disclosure, allowing the mechanics to carry the same tension as the narrative.

HOW I MADE IT

I built the experience as a functional first-person VR prototype with controller-based navigation and object interaction. Development included NPC orientation, animated emotion figures, shader and lighting work, collision behavior, and frame-by-frame visual effects.

Simulator tests revealed inconsistent scene tone, perspective tracking, and text occlusion. I revised these elements before testing the experience in the headset.

FORMATIVE PLAYTEST

A formative playtest with three participants suggested that the emotional-transparency premise and embodied interactions were engaging. Participants also identified abrupt transitions between story events and limited audio design.

These observations define the next iteration: smoother narrative transitions, a clearer relationship between sound and exposed emotion, and a larger study focused on how players interpret the ethical tension. The current feedback is directional, not evidence that the experience changes attitudes or relationships.
