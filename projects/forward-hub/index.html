NUS Forward Hub

This is the final project for the NUS course “CDE5311: Designing UX/UI for Human-Centred AI.” NUS Forward Hub is an AI-assisted campus marketplace for students to buy, sell, give away, and exchange second-hand items.

Team Project: Lee Zi Ee (Patrick), Chen Ray, and Tingxiao Wu

Human-Centred AI · UX/UI Design · Usability Testing

View the original Behance presentation ↗

THE GAP BETWEEN MOVING OUT AND MOVING IN

Students arriving on campus need affordable furniture and daily essentials, while graduating students often need to pass those items on before they leave. Existing resale channels separate these two moments, creating a timing gap between supply and demand.

Our journey analysis also identified trust, unreliable listings, scheduling, item dimensions, and meeting safety as connected problems. The design question became how a university-centred service could coordinate the entire exchange rather than simply display more listings.

DESIGNING AROUND THE STUDENT JOURNEY

We used Jing, an incoming NUS student preparing her accommodation before reaching Singapore, to connect the research to a concrete journey. She needs to discover relevant items remotely, compare their condition and fit, coordinate collection, and know whether the other person can be trusted.

The resulting concept combines NUS identity verification, seller profiles and ratings, comparison tools, in-app communication, and campus-oriented collection. Requests for unavailable items help incoming students signal demand before a suitable listing appears.

USING AI AT MOMENTS OF DECISION

AI is positioned as assistance within the marketplace flow. On the homepage, recommendations draw on a student's profile, preferences, owned items, activity, and market conditions. On a product page, comparison helps users weigh price and condition, while an AR try-on concept helps them judge fit in their space.

For sellers, image recognition proposes item details during listing and market signals help frame when and what to sell. These features reduce repeated searching and manual entry while keeping the student responsible for the final decision.

ONE LOOP FOR BUYERS AND SELLERS

The buyer flow links product discovery, requests, comparison, communication, payment, and rating. The seller flow supports AI-assisted listing, free-item sharing, trade management, and reviewing demand and reputation.

Community posts and messaging sit inside the same system so that advice, negotiation, and the transaction do not have to be reconstructed across unrelated apps. The prototype expresses this as a connected mobile experience rather than a set of independent AI tools.

TESTING AND REVISING THE PROTOTYPE

We tested the prototype with 14 participants—six buyers and eight sellers—using think-aloud tasks, a feedback survey, and the System Usability Scale. The project reported an overall SUS score of 80.9/100, with ease of use and the student-centred trust model emerging as strengths.

The findings led to more preference checkpoints during onboarding, more direct personalized actions on the homepage, and clearer market-demand and rating information for sellers. These revisions show how the final interface changed after testing; the marketplace and AI services remain a course-project prototype rather than a deployed NUS platform.
