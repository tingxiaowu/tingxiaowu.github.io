Emotion: The Transparent Realm

What if private feelings became characters that others could see? Emotion: The Transparent Realm is a narrative VR game that turns this premise into encounters about disclosure, privacy, and intimacy.

Project Lead: Tingxiao Wu

Virtual Reality · Critical Design · Narrative Interaction

WHEN PRIVATE FEELINGS BECOME PUBLIC

The game starts from a conflict rather than a promise of better emotion recognition: knowing more about another person's feelings does not necessarily give us permission to expose them. The fictional AI makes this conflict unavoidable by revealing feelings the characters have chosen to keep private.

The project asks a critical question: does making every emotion visible necessarily create a more honest or caring relationship?

THREE RELATIONSHIPS, ONE ESCALATING CONFLICT

The story externalizes private feelings as visible characters. They interrupt conversations and confront the player, turning something internal into a presence that occupies the same space. This gives emotional disclosure a consequence the player encounters rather than merely reads about.

The experience moves through three relationship-based levels—friendship, family, and romantic partnership—before a final confrontation. Each stage introduces a familiar social tension and asks the player to balance honesty, self-protection, and the expectations that sustain a close relationship.

TURNING DISCLOSURE INTO ACTION

Tracked controllers turn emotional conflict into embodied action. Players move through the scene, search for objects, grab and examine clues, comfort characters through gesture, dodge emotion figures, and face the final source of exposure.

Comforting, approaching, and avoiding become physical responses to the revealed emotion. The progression from conversation to spatial confrontation connects the controller interactions to the central question of how to respond when someone else's private feelings become visible.

BUILDING THE VR EXPERIENCE

The experience was developed as a first-person VR prototype with controller-based navigation and object interaction. Its implementation brings together NPC orientation, animated emotion figures, shaders and lighting, collisions, and frame-by-frame visual effects.

The development account identifies scene tone, perspective tracking, and text occlusion as iteration issues before the headset demo. These details matter because the player needs to follow both a conversation and the emotion figures entering the scene.

WHAT THE FIRST PLAYTEST REVEALED

A formative playtest with three participants suggested that the emotional-transparency premise and embodied interactions were engaging. Participants also identified abrupt transitions between story events and limited audio design.

The feedback suggests a specific next step: make the transitions and audio communicate why each revelation follows the previous encounter. With only three participants, the playtest informs iteration; it does not establish broader effects on attitudes or relationships.
